## 3. Primary target — Information Economics and Policy

### Current official scope evidence

Current Elsevier journal description states that IEP publishes peer-reviewed policy-oriented research on the production, distribution, and use of information and explicitly includes the role of information and information technology in the functioning of markets. It accepts theoretical and empirical research and publishes research papers, short contributions, and surveys.

Official scope URL:
https://shop.elsevier.com/journals/information-economics-and-policy/0167-6245

Accessed: 2026-09-23.

### Demonstrated topical fit

Recent close papers include:

- Umezawa (2022), *Behavior-Based Price Discrimination in a Horizontally and Vertically Differentiated Duopoly with Switching Costs*, IEP 61, 101004.
- Shrivastav (2023), *Information, Mis-Information, and History-Based Price Discrimination in a Duopoly*, IEP 65, 101059.
- Colombo, Graziano & Pignataro (2024), *Imperfect History-Based Price Discrimination with Asymmetric Market Shares*, IEP 67, 101092.

These publications demonstrate that history-based pricing, switching costs, consumer recognition, asymmetric shares, equilibrium configuration, profits, consumer surplus, and welfare are squarely inside the journal's recent editorial neighborhood.

### Fit assessment

**High topical fit / moderate contribution risk.**

Strengths:

- exact subject alignment;
- mathematical theory is explicitly within scope;
- welfare and information-market implications are central to the journal;
- recent close papers use related BBPD/switching-cost primitives;
- correction changes which source welfare comparisons are equilibrium comparisons.

Principal desk/referee risk:

- the theorem is deliberately MODEL-SPECIFIC;
- this is a reassessment of an existing benchmark rather than a new general BBPD model;
- policy implications must be stated as within-model implications rather than broad empirical claims.

The correct response is not to generalize the frozen theory. It is to position the paper as a rigorous reassessment that materially changes how an established information-pricing benchmark can be used.
