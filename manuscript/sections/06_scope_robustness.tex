\section{Strategy domains, robustness, and formal verification}
\label{sec:scope}

\subsection{Uniform-price strategy domain}

Proposition~\ref{prop:uniform} is unchanged whether uniform prices are allowed on all of \(\mathbb R\) or restricted to \(p_i\ge c\). A negative-margin action with positive demand is strictly improved by moving to zero margin, and a zero-margin action with positive demand admits a sufficiently small positive price increase. Zero-demand profiles require a joint argument: if the full-demand rival has a nonpositive margin, that rival can improve; if it has a positive margin, the zero-demand firm can match that positive margin, obtain positive demand, and earn positive profit. Appendix~\ref{app:uniform-proof} gives the complete argument. This reduction is specific to the uniform-price game and should not be silently transferred to the history-based game.

\subsection{Strong-HBP price selection}

For nondegenerate strong dominance, \(\bar x(s)\le x_0<1\), the accepted source member has \(q_A^d=c\). If below-cost poaching prices are admissible, however, there is a bounded family of zero-sales actions. Let
\[
u=\frac{q_A^d-c}{\tau}.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}
3-s-4x_0\le u\le0,
\qquad
\frac{p_B^d-c}{\tau}=u+2x_0-1+s.
\label{eq:strong-family}
\end{equation}
Across this nondegenerate family, firm \(A\)'s poaching price still attracts no \(B\)-history consumers. The allocation and real resource costs are unchanged, but the price paid by retained \(B\)-history consumers changes. Consumer surplus and the distribution of firm profits therefore depend on \(u\), while total welfare does not.

Under the nonnegative-margin restriction \(q_A^d\ge c\), the interval in \eqref{eq:strong-family} selects the source member \(u=0\) for \(x_0<1\). Equation~\eqref{eq:strong-cs} and the consumer-surplus interpretation of accepted Result~6 use that selection.

At \(x_0=1\), the inherited \(B\)-history segment has zero measure. The prices \(q_A\) and \(p_B\), which are directed only to that empty segment, are payoff-irrelevant and remain indeterminate even under nonnegative margins. Active-segment allocation, profits, consumer surplus, and welfare are invariant to those unused prices. Proposition~\ref{prop:strong-welfare} therefore remains well defined at the endpoint even though the full four-price vector is not unique.

\subsection{Normalization and portability}

The exact threshold \(x_H\) is invariant to a common translation in marginal cost \(c\) and to the transport-scale normalization. In dimensional prices, the equilibrium vector is always
\[
p_A^u=c+\tau+\sigma/3,
\qquad
p_B^u=c+\tau-\sigma/3.
\]
The direct clipped-demand representation and the price-difference representation also yield the same correspondence, and the allocation convention for a measure-zero set of indifferent consumers is immaterial.

These invariances do not turn Proposition~\ref{prop:uniform} into a theorem about arbitrary switching-cost Bertrand games. The proof uses the endpoint Hotelling geometry, the inherited split at \(x_0\), linear transport costs, full coverage, and the resulting five-piece demand. The contribution is deliberately model-specific.

\subsection{Interpretation}

The model isolates how inherited customer histories and switching costs create a discrete global pricing option: a firm can stop poaching and move to a plateau boundary while retaining its inherited consumers. The analysis is theoretical. We make no empirical causal claim, calibration claim, or policy claim beyond the comparative welfare statements inside the maintained source model.
