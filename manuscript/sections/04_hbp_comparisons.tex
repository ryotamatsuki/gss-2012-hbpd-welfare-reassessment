\section{Consumer surplus and profit comparisons}
\label{sec:comparisons}

The equilibrium correction changes the domain on which the two pricing regimes can be compared, but it does not by itself determine the sign of consumer surplus or profits. Those objects must be recomputed from the realized allocation.

\subsection{Market shares}

At the uniform candidate, the relevant \(A\)-history cutoff is
\[
x_u=\frac12+\frac{s}{6}.
\]
Whenever the uniform profile is a pure equilibrium, Proposition~\ref{prop:uniform} implies \(x_0\ge x_H(s)>x_u(s)\). Thus only \(A\)-history consumers in \((x_u,x_0]\) switch to \(B\), and firm \(A\)'s equilibrium share is \(x_u\).

On the accepted weak-HBP branch, firm \(A\)'s share is \((2-x_0)/3\). Hence, whenever the two pure equilibria coexist,
\begin{equation}
x_u-\frac{2-x_0}{3}
=
\frac{2x_0+s-1}{6}>0.
\label{eq:share-gap}
\end{equation}
The qualitative market-share ranking in accepted Result~2 therefore survives, although the weak-branch displayed difference in the accepted manuscript does not match its own equilibrium shares.

\subsection{Weak-dominance consumer surplus}

The uniform profile has two possible allocation branches even before one asks whether it is an equilibrium. If \(x_0\ge x_u\), some \(A\)-history consumers switch to \(B\). If \(x_0<x_u\), no consumer switches and the inherited allocation remains unchanged. Integrating realized utility over the clipped allocation gives the following profile comparison.

\begin{proposition}[Branch-correct weak-HBP consumer surplus]
\label{prop:weak-cs}
For the accepted weak-HBP price profile and the accepted uniform-price profile,
\[
CS^d-CS^u=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle
\frac{-\tau^2(52x_0^2-52x_0-1)
+2\sigma\tau(18x_0-17)+\sigma^2}{36\tau},
&x_0\ge x_u,\\[1.2em]
\displaystyle
\frac{\sigma^2+12\sigma\tau x_0-14\sigma\tau
-8\tau^2x_0^2+8\tau^2x_0+5\tau^2}{18\tau},
&x_0<x_u.
\end{cases}
\]
The two expressions agree at \(x_0=x_u\), and \(CS^d-CS^u>0\) throughout the accepted weak-dominance profile domain \(1/2<x_0<\bar x(s)\).
\end{proposition}

The switching-branch expression is exactly accepted Eq.~(15) after distributing its outer minus sign. Thus Eq.~(15) itself is not the source error. Two separate problems follow it. First, after substituting \(\sigma=s\tau\), the accepted Eq.~(16) display introduces the opposite sign. Second, Eq.~(13), and therefore Eq.~(15), describes the one-way-switch allocation and cannot be extended into \(x_0<x_u\), where the switching interval is reversed and realized demand is no-switch.

The exact point \((s,x_0)=(0.99,0.501)\) lies on the no-switch branch. Direct integration gives
\[
CS^d-CS^u=\frac{17993}{4500000}>0,
\]
whereas the out-of-domain extension of accepted Eq.~(15) gives
\[
-\frac{103039}{4500000}<0.
\]
At the valid switching-branch point \((s,x_0)=(0.1,0.7)\), accepted Eq.~(15) and direct primitive integration both give
\[
CS^d-CS^u=\frac{221}{720}>0.
\]
These two regressions separate source fidelity from allocation-branch validity.

\begin{corollary}[Weak-HBP equilibrium consumer surplus]
\label{cor:weak-cs}
Whenever weak HBP and uniform pricing both admit the pure equilibria being compared,
\[
CS^d>CS^u.
\]
Thus the consumer-surplus sign reversal stated in accepted Result~3 is not reproduced on the corrected pure-equilibrium domain.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{The smaller firm's profit under weak dominance}

The accepted Eq.~(22) uses the switching allocation of the uniform candidate. For profile comparisons outside the pure-equilibrium overlap, the uniform candidate must instead be evaluated on its realized branch. Direct demand gives
\[
\frac{\pi_A^d-\pi_A^u}{\tau}
=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle
\frac{s^2+12sx_0-10s+20x_0^2-20x_0+1}{18},
&x_0\ge x_u,\\[0.9em]
\displaystyle
\frac{s^2+3sx_0-2s+10x_0^2-19x_0+5}{9},
&x_0<x_u,
\end{cases}
\]
and
\begin{equation}
\frac{\pi_B^d-\pi_B^u}{\tau}
=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle
\frac{s^2-12sx_0+14s+20x_0^2-20x_0+1}{18},
&x_0\ge x_u,\\[0.9em]
\displaystyle
\frac{s^2-9sx_0+7s+10x_0^2-x_0-4}{9},
&x_0<x_u.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:B-profit-gap}
\end{equation}
Both firms' profit-gap formulas are continuous at \(x_0=x_u\).

\begin{proposition}[Smaller-firm profit on the weak pure overlap]
\label{prop:B-profit}
For every \(0\le s<s_c\) and every \(x_H(s)\le x_0<\bar x(s)\),
\[
\pi_B^d<\pi_B^u.
\]
\end{proposition}

On the pure overlap, \(x_0\ge x_H>x_u\), so the first branch of \eqref{eq:B-profit-gap} applies. Its numerator is convex in \(x_0\). On the larger interval \([1/2,\bar x]\), its endpoint values are
\[
s^2+8s-4<0
\quad\text{and}\quad
\frac{(1+s)(21s-11)}4<0
\]
for \(0\le s<s_c\). Convexity therefore implies negativity throughout the weak pure-equilibrium overlap.

Outside that overlap, accepted Eq.~(22) cannot simply be relabeled as a profile result. For example, at
\[
(s,x_0)=\left(\frac12,\frac{51}{100}\right),
\]
the realized uniform candidate is on the no-switch branch and
\[
\pi_B^d=\frac{3221}{9000}\tau,\qquad
\pi_B^u=\frac{49}{120}\tau,
\]
so
\[
\pi_B^d-\pi_B^u=-\frac{227}{4500}\tau<0.
\]
Extending the switching-branch expression into the same point would instead give \(4\tau/375>0\). This sign reversal is precisely why Result~4 must be separated into realized profile branches and the smaller weak-domain pure-equilibrium overlap.

\subsection{What the equilibrium correction does}

The distinction between profile and equilibrium comparisons is central. Proposition~\ref{prop:weak-cs} is a statement about two specified price profiles on the entire source weak-dominance domain. Corollary~\ref{cor:weak-cs} and Proposition~\ref{prop:B-profit} are equilibrium statements only on
\[
0<s<s_c,
\qquad
x_H(s)\le x_0<\bar x(s).
\]
No consumer-surplus or profit value is attributed to an uncharacterized mixed uniform-pricing equilibrium below \(x_H\). The smaller-firm loss in Proposition~\ref{prop:B-profit} is a weak-dominance result only; it is not extended to strong dominance, where the source-selected HBP profit comparison can have the opposite sign.
