\section{Global proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:uniform}}
\label{app:uniform-proof}

This appendix closes the pure-strategy proof by treating the degenerate cases first and then exhausting every branch for \(0<s<1\) and \(1/2<x_0<1\). Normalize \(\tau=1\).

\paragraph{The case \(s=0\).}
When switching is costless, inherited histories do not affect choice and
\[
m_A(d)=\clip\!\left(\frac{d+1}{2},0,1\right).
\]
Full capture cannot be Nash by the margin--demand argument below. On the interior branch, the two strictly concave unilateral profit problems have first-order conditions \(a=d+1\) and \(b=1-d\). Together with \(d=b-a\), these give the unique profile \(d=0\), \(a=b=1\).

\paragraph{The endpoint \(x_0=1\).}
When the inherited \(B\)-history segment has zero measure,
\[
m_A(d)=\clip\!\left(\frac{d+1+s}{2},0,1\right).
\]
The smooth interior first-order conditions yield
\[
d^*=-\frac{2s}{3},\qquad
a^*=1+\frac{s}{3},\qquad
b^*=1-\frac{s}{3}.
\]
Against \(b^*\), the best full-\(A\)-capture boundary is \(d=1-s\), where \(A\)'s payoff is \(2s/3\); the source payoff exceeds it by \((3-s)^2/18>0\). Against \(a^*\), every no-demand action of \(B\) yields zero, whereas the source action yields \((3-s)^2/18>0\). The switching pieces are strictly concave and have the displayed interior vertices. Hence the source profile is the unique pure equilibrium at \(x_0=1\). This separate argument is needed because the usual plateau gain \(2s(1-x_0)\) vanishes at this endpoint.

For the remainder assume
\[
0<s<1,\qquad \frac12<x_0<1.
\]
Write
\[
L=-1-s,\qquad
\alpha=2x_0-1-s,\qquad
\gamma=2x_0-1+s,\qquad
U=1+s.
\]
On the five demand pieces in \eqref{eq:five-piece-demand},
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{region} & \pi_A(d;b) & \pi_B(d;a)\\
\hline
d\le L & 0 & a+d\\
L<d<\alpha & (b-d)(d-L)/2 & (a+d)(1-s-d)/2\\
\alpha\le d\le\gamma & x_0(b-d) & (1-x_0)(a+d)\\
\gamma<d<U & (b-d)(d+1-s)/2 & (a+d)(1+s-d)/2\\
d\ge U & b-d & 0
\end{array}
\]
Every switching-branch payoff is a concave quadratic and every plateau payoff is affine. A global best response must therefore occur at a feasible quadratic vertex or at a regime endpoint.

\paragraph{Margins, demand, and capture tails.}
The unrestricted real-price strategy space is handled jointly with demand rather than by assuming that an excluded firm can always enter at a positive margin.

If \(d\le L\), \(A\) has zero demand and \(B\) serves the whole market. If \(b<0\), \(B\) strictly improves by choosing margin zero and obtaining nonnegative profit. If \(b=0\), a sufficiently small increase in \(b\) preserves positive demand and yields strictly positive profit. If \(b>0\), \(A\) can choose
\[
a'=\frac{b+1+s}{2}>0,
\]
which moves \(d\) strictly above \(L\) and gives \(A\) positive demand and positive profit. Thus full capture by \(B\) is never Nash. The case \(d\ge U\) is symmetric: if \(a<0\) or \(a=0\), the capturing firm itself improves; if \(a>0\), \(B\) can enter with \(b'=(a+1+s)/2>0\).

More generally, a firm serving positive demand at a negative margin strictly improves by charging \(c\), and a zero margin with positive demand is strictly improved by a sufficiently small positive price increase. Hence every remaining pure-equilibrium candidate has positive margins and positive demand for both firms.

\paragraph{No-switch plateau and both kinks.}
On \(\alpha\le d\le\gamma\), \(A\)'s payoff \(x_0(b-d)\) decreases in \(d\), whereas \(B\)'s payoff \((1-x_0)(a+d)\) increases in \(d\). Therefore no plateau interior can be Nash. At \(d=\alpha\), \(B\) can move to \(d=\gamma\), keep share \(1-x_0\), and gain \(2s(1-x_0)>0\), which is strict because \(x_0<1\). At \(d=\gamma\), \(A\) can move to \(d=\alpha\), keep share \(x_0\), and gain \(2sx_0>0\). Thus neither kink is Nash.

\paragraph{Lower switching branch.}
For \(L<d<\alpha\), the two first-order conditions are
\[
a=d+1+s,\qquad b=1-s-d.
\]
Together with \(d=b-a\), they yield
\[
d^*=-\frac{2s}{3},\qquad a^*=1+\frac{s}{3},\qquad b^*=1-\frac{s}{3}.
\]
The branch condition is \(x_0>x_u(s)=1/2+s/6\).

Against \(b^*\), \(A\)'s lower-branch quadratic has its unique maximum at \(d^*\). Its best plateau payoff occurs at \(d=\alpha\), and
\[
\frac{\pi_A(a^*,b^*)-\pi_A(\alpha;b^*)}{\tau}=2(x_0-x_u)^2>0.
\]
On the reverse-switch branch, \(A\)'s quadratic vertex is \(d=s/3\). Since
\[
\gamma=2x_0-1+s>s>\frac{s}{3},
\]
the derivative is strictly negative throughout \(d\ge\gamma\); its best value is therefore the left endpoint and is bounded by the plateau comparison.

Against \(a^*\), \(B\)'s best plateau action is the no-poaching kink \(d=\gamma\), with
\[
b_k=a^*+\gamma=2x_0+\frac{4s}{3}.
\]
The reverse-switch quadratic for \(B\) also has vertex \(d=s/3<\gamma\), so it is strictly decreasing throughout its feasible interval. The only remaining global comparison is
\[
\frac{\pi_B(a^*,b^*)-\pi_B(a^*,b_k)}{\tau}
=
2\bigl(x_0-x_L(s)\bigr)\bigl(x_0-x_H(s)\bigr).
\]
Because \(x_L<1/2<x_0\), \(b^*\) is a global best response if and only if \(x_0\ge x_H(s)\).

At equality \(x_0=x_H(s)\), \(B\) is indifferent between \(b^*\) and \(b_k\). The kink profile \((a^*,b_k)\) is not Nash: \(A\) can move the price difference from \(\gamma\) to \(\alpha\), raise its margin by \(2s\), keep share \(x_0\), and gain \(2sx_0>0\).

\paragraph{Upper switching branch.}
For \(\gamma<d<U\), the two first-order conditions imply
\[
d=\frac{2s}{3},\qquad
a=1-\frac{s}{3},\qquad
b=1+\frac{s}{3}.
\]
Feasibility would require \(x_0<1/2-s/6\), contradicting \(x_0>1/2\). Hence no pure equilibrium arises on the reverse-switch interior.

\paragraph{Conclusion.}
For \(0<s<1\) and \(1/2<x_0<1\), capture tails, nonpositive-margin candidates, the plateau, both kinks, and the reverse-switch interior have been excluded. If \(x_0\le x_u\), the source candidate lies on or inside the no-switch plateau and is not Nash. If \(x_u<x_0<x_H\), \(B\) strictly prefers the no-poaching kink. If \(x_0\ge x_H\), both source actions are global best responses. Together with the separately treated cases \(s=0\) and \(x_0=1\), this proves Proposition~\ref{prop:uniform} on the full maintained domain.

The same margin--demand argument proves that allowing all real uniform prices does not create an additional pure equilibrium. Thus the pure-equilibrium set is identical under unrestricted prices and under \(p_i\ge c\).
